The safety story in one sentence: nothing touches the page without your
stamp, and everything stops on your halt.

## How a run works

Press RUN and the mind plans up to 12 steps. Read-only steps — reading the
tab, snapshotting the page — run freely. The moment the plan calls for hands
(navigate, click, type), the desk checks for a live STAMP. Stamped? It acts
and logs the action. Not stamped? Blocked, logged, run continues without it.

## Why twelve steps

Twelve is a ceiling, not a target. The loop cannot spin forever, burn your
key budget overnight, or wander off into step 147 of a plan nobody reads.
Short plans stay legible. Legible plans stay safe.

## HALT is the fire escape

Big red habit. HALT kills the loop mid-step, disarms the hands, and leaves
the full log on screen so you can see exactly where it stopped. Drill it once
— stamp, run, halt — so your thumb knows the way before you ever need it.

Trust the mechanism, not the marketing. Stamp gates the hands. Halt kills the
loop. Twelve caps the damage. That's the whole story, and it's enforced in
code, not promised in copy.
